Why Santa Teresa businesses use virtual tours

Santa Teresa is remote and everybody involved knows it. Getting there takes a ferry or a long drive on roads that are famously rough, which means a guest is not making a casual booking — they are committing to a journey before they ever see the property.

That raises the value of showing the place properly. When arriving is an effort, the questions people need answered beforehand get more serious: is this worth the trip, what is the room actually like, how far is it from the beach, what is around it. A walkthrough answers those in a way that a set of well-lit photographs, however good, cannot fully settle.

Working in Santa Teresa

The honest constraint in Santa Teresa is access. It is a long trip from our base in La Fortuna, and that is reflected transparently as a travel line on the quote rather than folded invisibly into the price. In practice it also means we plan Santa Teresa work as a single thorough visit rather than a quick one, and it is worth capturing more than you think you need while we are there.

Technically the coast behaves much like the rest of the Pacific: very strong light, deep shade under roofs, and salt and dust in the air. Scenes are bracketed and hand-stitched, and exterior and drone work is scheduled toward the ends of the day.

About Santa Teresa & Mal País

Santa Teresa sits at the southern tip of the Nicoya Peninsula, running into the neighbouring communities of Playa Carmen and Mal País. The area is known for:

  • Beaches: Playa Santa Teresa and Playa Carmen, consistent surf along an unusually long stretch of sand
  • Cabo Blanco: The Cabo Blanco Absolute Natural Reserve, established in 1963 as the country’s first protected area
  • Accommodation: A dense concentration of design-led boutique hotels, villas and wellness properties
  • Access: Reached by ferry across the Gulf of Nicoya or by road around it — part of what keeps the area quiet

The remoteness that limits the volume of visitors is also what shapes the market: fewer guests, staying longer, spending more, and deciding largely on what they can see before they travel.

Do you travel to Santa Teresa and Mal País?

Yes. It is one of the longer trips we make from La Fortuna, so travel is quoted as its own clearly stated line and we plan the shoot as a single comprehensive visit.

Is it worth capturing more scenes in a remote location?

Usually, yes. The marginal cost of additional scenes during a visit is far lower than the cost of returning, so remote properties tend to get better value from a fuller capture.

Can you shoot both the property and the surrounding area?

Yes. Drone 360° aerials are particularly useful here, because context — where the property sits relative to the beach and the town — is a large part of what a remote guest is trying to work out.
