Case study · Osa Peninsula

El Genio Estate

A six-bedroom private estate in the Osa jungle, photographed end to end for an audience that books it without ever seeing it first.

The brief

A property nobody views first

El Genio sits on the Osa Peninsula, which is about as far from a casual viewing as Costa Rica gets. Guests fly in from North America and Europe having committed to a week at a property they have only seen on a screen, and buyers at this level are making the shortlist that justifies the trip in the first place.

That puts an unusual load on the photography. It is not decorating a listing that a viewing will settle later — it is the viewing. It has to carry the rooms, the light, the scale of the place, and the parts of the estate that no interior frame can reach: the walk down through the mangroves, the dock, the way the buildings sit in the trees.

The walkthrough

Where the stills stop

Thirty-nine connected panoramas, hand-stitched at 12K, with navigation between spaces and information hotspots throughout. On a property of this size a guest cannot hold the layout in their head from a photo set — which building the dorms are in, how far the pool is from the great room, what is at the bottom of the boardwalk.

That is the quiet value of a complete walkthrough: it settles the questions that otherwise arrive as messages, and it removes the suspicion a highlight reel always invites, which is what got left out.

Common questions

About this shoot

What was photographed at El Genio Estate?

The full property: every bedroom and bathroom, the great room, kitchen, games room, pool deck, the grotto, the boardwalk down to the mangroves and the private dock, plus drone aerials of the estate in its setting and a twilight exterior.

Why does a villa like this need both stills and a 360 tour?

Stills win the click on a booking site or a portal, where a guest is scanning dozens of properties. The tour answers what the stills cannot: how the buildings relate to each other, how far the water is, what is around the corner. On a property this size the layout is genuinely hard to read from photographs alone.

How long did the shoot take?

A full day on site for the stills and aerials, with the twilight exterior at the end of it, and a second pass for the 360 scenes. A property of this scale is not a half-day job and pricing it as one shows in the result.

What does a shoot like this cost?
