The problem is not the tools. It is knowing where to point them

Most owners have already tried the chatbots. The question that actually matters is narrower and harder: out of everything your team does in a week, which parts are worth handing to a machine, what does that cost to build, and what does it save once it is running?

That is the whole point of starting with an audit rather than a project. Two weeks of looking at how the work really flows is usually enough to separate the three workflows that are genuinely worth automating from the fifteen that are not. Sometimes the honest answer is that your bottleneck is a staffing or pricing problem and no amount of AI will touch it. We would rather tell you that for $950 than discover it together at $15,000.

What we automate

The work that quietly eats your week

We start with the repetitive, high-volume, low-judgement work — the tasks where a machine is genuinely better than a person having a bad afternoon, and where the savings are easy to measure.

Enquiry and booking replies

The same twenty questions, in English and Spanish, answered instantly at any hour — with a clean handoff to a human the moment a conversation stops being routine.

Quotes, deposits and follow-up

Quotes drafted from your own rate card, deposits chased on a schedule, and nothing sitting in an inbox for four days because it was somebody’s day off.

Enquiry to CRM, without retyping

Messages from WhatsApp, email, Instagram and your forms turned into structured records with source, dates, party size and value already filled in.

Listing, site and social copy

Property and tour descriptions, OTA listings and captions drafted in your voice and in both languages, from your own material rather than generic filler.

Review and feedback digests

Every review across every platform pulled into a weekly read of what guests actually keep mentioning, separated from the noise.

Assistants trained on your content

An assistant that has read your rates, policies, menus, directions and tour details — so it answers from your business rather than from the open internet.

How we work

Audit, build, then run it

Three stages. You can stop after any of them, and plenty of clients do.

1 · Audit

Two weeks. We watch how the work actually happens, then hand back a ranked roadmap: each workflow with the hours it eats, what automating it costs, and what it saves. Yours to keep, and yours to take elsewhere if you want.

2 · Build

We build the top of that list at a fixed price agreed up front, inside the tools your team already uses. You get runbooks and a walkthrough, not a black box only we can maintain.

3 · Run

Automations drift when prices, seasons and models change. A retainer keeps them current and adds the next workflow. Optional — if your team wants to own it, the handover is written for that.

Why us

We already know how your business runs

We have spent years inside Costa Rican hotels, retreats, restaurants and property portfolios building virtual tours and Street View. That means we have watched how bookings really arrive, where enquiries stall, and which spreadsheet everything secretly depends on.

That context is most of the job. A generalist AI consultant has to spend the first month learning what an OTA is, why the shoulder season changes everything, and why your reservations manager does not trust the booking engine. We start from the workflow, not from the technology — which is also why we will happily tell you when the answer is a better process rather than a model.
